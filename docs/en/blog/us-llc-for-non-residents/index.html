The US LLC for non-residents, an honest guide

What a US LLC really does for you as a non-resident, when it makes sense and what it doesn't do. A clear guide, no hype and no fine print.

Nordwise·2 min read

If you invoice online from outside the US, someone has probably told you to "open an LLC and stop paying taxes". The first part can be a great idea. The second, put that way, is false. This guide covers what a US LLC actually does for you, and what it doesn't.

What an LLC actually is

An LLC (Limited Liability Company) is the most flexible business entity in the United States. For a non-resident with no physical presence in the US, it works as a pass-through vehicle, the LLC itself pays no US corporate tax, profits flow to the owner.

That makes it light and cheap to maintain, but it also means something important, taxes are paid where you are tax resident. Your country of residence still taxes what you earn through the LLC.

So what is it good for?

More than you might think:

  • Frictionless payments. Stripe, PayPal Business and US payment rails work better, with fewer blocks and more client trust.
  • First-class banking. Mercury, Relay or Wise Business with your LLC and EIN, no travel required.
  • Asset protection. It separates your personal assets from the business.
  • Image and market access. Invoicing as a US company opens doors with American clients.
  • Privacy. In states like New Mexico, members don't appear in public records.

What your tax authority expects

An LLC managed from your home country usually needs to be declared properly there, and the income it generates is typically taxed in your personal return. None of this is a problem if it's planned, the problem is finding out two years too late.

The LLC is not a trick to stop paying taxes. It's a tool for structure, payments and protection that, used well, is perfectly legal and enormously useful.

When does it make sense?

In our experience, an LLC is a particularly good fit if:

  1. You sell services or digital products to international clients.
  2. You suffer blocks or limits with local payment processors.
  3. You want to separate your business from your personal assets.
  4. You're also considering a tax residency change in the medium term.
